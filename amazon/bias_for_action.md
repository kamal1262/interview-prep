
## **STAR+HR Example (Bias for Action)**

**Headline**  
I quickly identified and solved a major gap in our property search engine at iProperty Group, reducing null search results by 50% within two weeks and protecting both customer experience and company revenue.  
Let me explain in more detail.

**Situation**  
In 2017, I was working as a Data Engineer at iProperty Group in Malaysia. Our platform had ~30,000 daily users and processed ~10 million property searches monthly. I noticed that many user searches were returning no results due to typos, abbreviations like “PJ” for Petaling Jaya, and other variations. This was frustrating for customers and wasting backend resources.

**Task**  
Although it wasn’t my direct responsibility, I realized this was a critical customer and business problem. If left unsolved, it could damage user trust, reduce engagement, and increase costs. My goal was to quickly deliver an improvement to customer experience, while also setting up a long-term scalable solution.

**Action**

- I dove into search logs and found ~40–50% of queries (~4M/month) returned null results.
    
- I proposed a two-phase approach:
    
    - **Quick Fix:** Build a mapping for the top 30–35 keywords/typos covering ~80% of failed searches. Implemented in 2 weeks.
        
    - **Long-Term Fix:** Designed and deployed a machine learning–based semantic search solution to handle future unknown variations. Took ~1.5 months.
        
- I presented this data and plan to the product manager, secured approval, and led implementation with the engineering team.
    

**Result**

- Reduced null results from ~4M to ~2M per month (50% reduction).
    
- Improved customer experience by ensuring even typo or short-form queries returned results.
    
- Reduced wasted backend processing costs.
    
- Helped increase engagement — customers stayed on the platform longer as their searches were more successful.
    

**Reflection**  
This experience reinforced two lessons:

1. Sometimes a fast “80% solution” is more valuable than waiting for a perfect fix.
    
2. Following up with a scalable long-term solution is critical to avoid repeating the problem.  
    Since then, I’ve applied this bias-for-action mindset in other projects, balancing urgency with scalability.
